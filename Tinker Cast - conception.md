Initially, I came up with my [[#Cognitive Tinker Frame]] framework as something that I thought hit a **niche** that Building a Second Brain, Zettelkasten, Linking Your Thinking and everything else doesn't really get into - the use of ==*science*== by regular people. ==*Tinkering*==, like the science we learn about in history, not the (theoretically) high-quality peer-reviewed large-scale double-blind science we expect today.

I won't single out which community I've seen this in, but "**do what works for you**" was a common response to the questions I was most excited to get real answers to. I mean, if I knew what worked for me, I wouldn't be asking a question, right? And when **I asked people how they know what worked for them**, the answer was usually 🤷 How weird is that?

So, where to start? Problems! Fun. So I listed out problems, some solutions, and prioritized solutions that solved multiple problems while doing other triage. That was nice, and then I was left with **problems that don't have an obvious solution**, including things for which I had [[CPTSD|trauma responses]] that complicated otherwise simple matters. (I mean, the trauma responses *themselves* but I have to live with many of them for now.)

I find it easiest to frame in terms of problems
- My cat is waking me up before my alarm
- My home's CO2 is [too high](https://www.theatlantic.com/science/archive/2019/12/carbon-dioxide-pollution-making-people-dumber-heres-what-we-know/603826/)
- I don't know what headache treatment works (best) for me

None of these has an obvious solution, but I can develop (ideally boolean/falsifiable) hypotheses
- An auto feeder an hour before my alarm will help
- Opening windows for a cross-draft will reliably decrease my Aranet4 readings
- I can compare two different treatments, by setting an alarm 30 minutes after trying them (by flipping a coin each time)

## In practice...

That sounds fairly straightforward, right? In theory, yes, it's quite straightforward. But in practice...

Applying [[My scientific method|science]] is unnatural to most of us, who want to just do whatever is intuitive in most moments. Interrupting our flow to deal with a timer or takes notes generally feels onerous, and I'm not too exceptional in this regard.

The [[My Tinker Cast]] I've been working on seeks to help with this problem, and my personal opinion is that it makes a *big difference*. I'm glad that I've focused more on building my digital system than evangelizing "raw" science, which can quite be tedious.
