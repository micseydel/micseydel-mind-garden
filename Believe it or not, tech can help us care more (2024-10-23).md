This is going to be a bit dense but I'll try to pick up where Annie Murphy Paul in 2021:

> [!abstract] The last sentence of *[The Extended Mind](https://anniemurphypaul.com/books/the-extended-mind/)*
> "Acknowledging the reality of the extended mind might well lead us to embrace the extended heart."

An "extended mind" example would be people playing **cyborg chess**, where [humans play chess against each](https://www.irishexaminer.com/opinion/commentanalysis/arid-30975938.html) other with access to chess AI engines. This may sound boring, but chess master Garry Kasparov had this [to say](https://www.wired.com/story/defeated-chess-champ-garry-kasparov-made-peace-ai/) in 2020:

> [!success] Garry Kasparov (chess master after which [Kasparov's law](https://news.northeastern.edu/2024/06/17/garry-kasparov-chess-humans-ai/) is named)
> "It was a mistake to think that if we develop very powerful chess machines, the game would be dull \[...] AlphaZero is totally the opposite. For me it was **complementary** \[...] It found that it could actually sacrifice material for aggressive action. **It’s not creative, it just sees the pattern, the odds**."

I believe that just like our extended minds can leverage complementary benefits from tech, our extended hearts can as well. But it's not just about our goals or intentions, sometimes we need *systems*:

> [!tip] James Clear ([Atomic Habits](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits))
> "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems" ([[We don't rise to our goals, we fall to our systems|ref]])

So, in this essay I will talk (briefly) about how to leverage our extended minds, our intelligence, to **care** more or more effectively. "Intelligence" is not the star of this show but it is useful, especially this:

> [!question] Jeff Hawkins ([A Thousand Brains](https://www.numenta.com/resources/books/a-thousand-brains-by-jeff-hawkins/))
> "The secret ingredient, if you will, is that intelligence is created through thousands of small models of the world, where each model uses reference frames to store knowledge and create behaviors." ([[The secret ingredient to intelligence|ref]])

To elaborate a little more on what Hawkins is referring to by "reference frames," you can think of a reference frame as tracking some *thing* in some *space*:
- a point in an (x, y) coordination system space (2D)
- where you parked in a parking lot (2D or 3D)
- who you plan to vote for in a democratic election (XD)

Ok, let's get back to the extended heart by quoting someone working on regrowing amputated limbs:

> [!example] Michael Levin ([An organicist talks about AI (not really about AI at all), and fear](https://thoughtforms.life/an-organicist-talks-about-ai-not-really-about-ai-at-all-and-fear/))
> "The goal of my work is fundamentally ethical and spiritual, not technological"

My goal here is to integrate some of what has resonated from these various experts and show how we can **care more** by extending our minds with **externalized reference frames**. For all the examples above, externalizing the final result could be writing on a sticky note or in a notes app, whatever works best for the individual in the particular situation.

![[Pasted image 20230105040315.png|360]]

Writing isn't the only low-tech way to extend our minds though. Another straightforward way is with analog timers. Someone can be more effective at doing laundry, baking, or whatever else by extending their mind with a simple timer.

A timer allows you to move your **attention** to something new, without giving up care for what you'll come back to when the time is right. A timer is a kind of externalized reference frame, tracking movement through time in instead of space as in the examples above. When time is up, the timer sends you a message that will let you change your behavior to be more consistent with your long-term intentions, with **who you want to be** when empowered.

We can think of the timer as reliable **externalized agency**, just like a sticky note is **externalized memory**. Combining these two principals can feel like magic!

By externalizing my mind and my care, I've made my process for taking care of Peanut fewer steps and more robust. All I have to do (most of the time) is make a voice note like "I just sifted poop from the back litter box" to add to the summary note. If he's yowling at 2am and I'm sleep deprived, I can open the daily litter sifting note, and if the totals are off I can investigate who produced the latest pee clump using notes that aren't (as) automatically organized.

Before building this custom system, I used Google Keep transcribed voice notes. This meant I had to choose between proactively organizing notes when I was able, or hoping I could organize them as needed. Both choices were painful in different ways and at different times. By externalizing these **steps** for computers to take for me, I have the best of both worlds - I don't need to manually organize my notes proactively, and when I need to do so reactively, it's already partly done. This gives me **peace of mind**.

I shared about my cats because that was the beginning of this journey for me, but a potentially more relatable example might be [H.A.L.T.](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/halt-hungry-angry-lonely-tired):

> HALT is an acronym that stands for “hunger, anger, lonely, tired.” To use the HALT method, you check in with yourself periodically and ask yourself how you’re feeling in these four categories. Sometimes, check-ins can occur daily. Other times, you may check in with yourself hourly. How often you participate in the HALT method is up to you.

Let's look at my atomic agents graphic again (🥲), with the cat stuff mostly cropped out (😿😿):

The pink speaker 🗣️ emoji's job is to send out the transcriptions for voice memos I take, to "listeners" like the cat listener, the brown ear 👂  You can think of that speaker as a kind of center, representing **me**. Or an eavesdropper gossiping about me 🤷

I have HALT agents for
- tracking when I **last ate** ("I just ate" voice memos, and a "smart" timer for reminders; salad+light blue circle to the left of the speaker)
- tracking anger/**distress** (word choice in voice memos; the ear+red circle below the speaker)
- **tiredness** (sleep data pulled from Fitbit at specific times; at the bottom)
- **CO2** tracking because it can impair cognition (from an Aranet4 at a regular interval; left of the sleep/Fitbit stuff)

If too much time passes without me eating, I get a push notification. If I seem distressed while thinking out loud, my smart lights flash and I get a push notification. If I'm tired or the CO2 is above 1000PPM... you get the picture. But if I'm hungry *and* tired, the notification specifically says, "Grab food and nap if possible."

By externalizing these various reference frames and then integrating them, I can provide custom messages and get a kind of HALT score. When that score reaches 3, the push notification suggests disengaging from whatever I'm doing to get that score down. HALT is a good idea, but it's like meditation - the more you need it, the harder it is to actually do.

Honestly, I've never felt more empowered by software in my whole life because merely HALTing can be so much work 😆 Obviously simple timers are incredibly useful, and tools like Google Calendar can be a game-changer, but there was no app for organizing notes on my cats (ChatGPT was not reliable when I tried). There might be a HALT app out there, but I'm not interested because I can't network them together like my atomic agents - I prefer flashing my lights to keep my eyes off my phone, for example, over push notifications.
